Kidnapping Is Paying Somebody. But Who?

October 24, 2026

Let’s be honest: kidnapping in Nigeria is no longer just random criminals taking people for ransom. It has become an economy of its own.

The National Bureau of Statistics estimated that Nigerians paid about ₦2.23 trillion in ransom between May 2023 and April 2024. That is an extraordinary amount of money moving through an illegal industry.

And this is where the uncomfortable question begins: can an industry worth trillions of naira operate on such a scale without some form of protection, facilitation or complicity within the system?

I am not saying every government official is involved. There is no evidence to justify such a blanket claim. But we cannot pretend the question does not deserve investigation.

Think about it. Kidnappers need information, communication, transportation, access to communities and ways to receive and move money. They also operate in places where government security agencies are supposed to be in control.

If the government truly wants to destroy this business, it must go beyond arresting kidnappers. It must follow the money, identify the financiers and expose anyone protecting them.

Because here is the uncomfortable truth: trillions of naira do not disappear into the hands of criminals without leaving footprints. If authorities cannot find the money, the bigger question is whether they are unwilling—or unable—to look in the right places.

And until those behind the money are exposed, Nigerians have every reason to ask:

Who is really protecting the kidnapping business?
