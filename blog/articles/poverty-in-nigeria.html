Why Nigerian Politicians Need Poverty More Than They Need Development

October 20, 2026

Over 133 million Nigerians were living in multidimensional poverty, according to Nigeria's National Bureau of Statistics (NBS).

There is an uncomfortable reality about Nigerian politics: poverty can be politically useful.

A poor citizen needs immediate help—money for food, school fees, medical bills or transport. Politicians understand this. That is why, especially during elections, we see cash, rice, clothing and other items distributed to struggling citizens.

But we should ask a harder question:

What if a politician gives you ₦10,000 because he does not want you to ask why you cannot earn ₦100,000?

That is the difference between poverty relief and development.

The Politics of Handouts

There is nothing wrong with helping vulnerable people. The problem begins when handouts replace development.

A politician distributes rice and becomes a hero. Another pays school fees for a few students and receives praise. Another gives small amounts of money or equipment.

Meanwhile, roads remain poor, schools remain inadequate, hospitals struggle, electricity remains unreliable and young people remain unemployed.

Then, four years later, the politician returns with another bag of rice.

That is not development. It is political dependence.

A ₦10,000 handout solves a problem temporarily. An economy that allows someone to earn ₦100,000 every month changes their life.

Poverty Creates Dependence

When citizens depend on politicians for jobs, contracts, school fees or financial assistance, loyalty can become more important than accountability.

The citizen stops asking, “What has this government achieved?” and starts asking, “What can this politician give me?”

That is how patronage survives.

Political appointments can become rewards for loyalty. Government contracts can become opportunities for political networks. Constituency projects can be presented as personal gifts even though they are funded with public money.

Citizens should not have to thank politicians for providing what government already owes them.

Instead of repeatedly distributing food, make agriculture productive enough for farmers to earn sustainable incomes.

Instead of giving graduates political appointments, create businesses and industries capable of employing them.

Relief treats the consequence. Development addresses the cause.

Poverty as a Political Tool

When poverty persists for decades despite enormous public resources, we must question whether it is always simply incompetence.

Poverty can be engineered into a political system.

A population struggling to survive is easier to control than a population that is economically independent, educated and confident enough to demand accountability.

A citizen who needs a politician's ₦10,000 is less powerful than a citizen who earns his own ₦100,000.

Poverty in Nigeria is engineered by politicians so that they can control the masses.

Nigerian politicians DO NOT SEE POVERTY AS A PROBLEM; INSTEAD, THEY USE IT AS A TOOL TO WIN ELECTIONS.

Over 63% of Nigerians were found to be multidimensionally poor.

In Nigeria, poverty is a tool.
