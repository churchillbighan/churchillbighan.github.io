How to Do Money Laundering in Nigeria Without Getting Caught

Written by Churchill A. Bighan | November 8, 2026

Let's play a little game.

Imagine you wake up tomorrow and discover that you have stolen $200 million. Yes, $200 million!

At an exchange rate of approximately ₦1,348 to a dollar, that's about ₦269.6 billion.

Now, congratulations, you are suddenly worth almost ₦270 billion. But there is one small problem: the money is stolen.

How exactly would you spend it?

Would you walk into a bank and deposit billions? Would you start buying houses, expensive cars and private jets? Would you start transferring millions into different accounts?

And more importantly, how would you explain the source of that money if the banks, the EFCC or other investigative authorities came knocking?

This is where things get interesting.

The process of disguising illegally obtained money to make it appear legitimate is called money laundering. In simple terms, it is an attempt to give dirty money a clean identity.

In Nigeria, this is a serious financial crime. The Money Laundering (Prevention and Prohibition) Act 2022 provides the legal framework for tackling it, while the EFCC investigates financial crimes and the NFIU analyses suspicious financial transactions.

Real estate, luxury goods and even seemingly legitimate businesses can be exploited to disguise the origins of criminal proceeds. But owning expensive properties or running a successful business does not automatically make someone a money launderer.

Now imagine that after all the stress of stealing $200 million and trying to make it look legitimate, one morning you wake up to find out that the EFCC has frozen your accounts.

The houses you bought are under investigation. The cars you proudly displayed are being seized. Your business premises are suddenly surrounded by investigators, and your name is all over the news.

And if you think this is just a story, look at what happened in the case of Nigeria's former Attorney-General, Abubakar Malami.

In 2026, Malami and members of his family faced money-laundering charges involving approximately ₦8.7 billion. In a separate forfeiture proceeding, a Federal High Court ordered the final forfeiture of 48 properties linked to him.

Think about that for a moment. Forty-eight properties!

To be clear, the forfeiture proceedings and criminal charges are separate legal matters. Malami has pleaded not guilty to the charges.

Yesterday, everybody wanted to be your friend. Today, your name is in the newspapers for an entirely different reason.

Your family is confused, your business empire is under pressure, and the wealth you thought would secure your future has become the centre of a legal battle.

Just like that, the life you thought you had secured with $200 million becomes a nightmare.

Because sometimes, the biggest price of stolen wealth is not losing the money. It's losing everything else along with it.
