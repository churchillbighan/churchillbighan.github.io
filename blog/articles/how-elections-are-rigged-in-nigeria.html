How Elections Are Rigged in Nigeria

Written by Churchill A. Bighan

Election rigging in Nigeria is not just a story people tell on social media. Over the years, political parties, observers and ordinary Nigerians have made serious allegations about what happens before, during and after voting.

Take Kano.

In 2018, pictures and reports circulated showing what appeared to be children voting during the Kano local government elections. The controversy became serious enough for INEC to investigate. INEC later said it could not establish that the children actually voted, while acknowledging problems surrounding the voter register.

Then there are allegations of “imported voters.” In the 2018 Osun rerun, the CUPP alleged that uncollected PVCs were being released to politicians who intended to use people brought from outside the area to vote. INEC rejected the allegation.

But here is something I personally witnessed.

I attended Tinubu's campaign rally last Saturday. Around 18,000 people were estimated to have come out to show solidarity. What caught my attention was the allegation that virtually everyone who attended was given ₦5,000.

I am not presenting that as proof that those people were paid to vote for anyone. But it raises an uncomfortable question: if thousands of people can allegedly be mobilised with ₦5,000 simply to attend a political gathering, what happens when that same money is offered for a vote?

₦5,000 multiplied by 18,000 people is ₦90 million.

And that is just one gathering.

So when Nigerians talk about vote buying, perhaps the question should not simply be whether votes can be bought.

Perhaps the question is whether we are willing to admit that votes can become commodities when poverty meets political power.
