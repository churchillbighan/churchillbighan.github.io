Some Nigerians Have Iranian and Israeli Citizenship! Steps on How You Can Get Yours.

WRITTEN BY CHURCHILL A. BIGHAN
14 November 2026

I recently discovered that some Nigerians may have acquired Iranian and Israeli citizenship without visiting an embassy, submitting documents or paying immigration fees.

The process is surprisingly simple.

If you are a Muslim in certain parts of Northern Nigeria, all you need to do is wave an Iranian flag, defend Iran passionately and argue with anyone who disagrees with you.

If you are a Christian, simply become Israel's unofficial spokesperson on Facebook, defend its government and treat anyone questioning its actions as an enemy.

Congratulations! Your Nigerian passport still says Nigeria, but your loyalty appears to have relocated.

On March 2, 2026, members of the Islamic Movement in Nigeria staged demonstrations across several Nigerian cities, including Kano, Kaduna and Sokoto, waving Iranian flags and expressing solidarity with Iran following the killing of its Supreme Leader, Ali Khamenei.

Now, there is nothing wrong with sympathising with people affected by war. What is amusing, however, is how quickly some Nigerians become emotionally invested in foreign conflicts while remaining relatively quiet about the problems around them.

We have insecurity, unemployment, struggling public hospitals, poor infrastructure and young graduates desperately searching for opportunities.

Yet some people have enough energy to spend entire days defending governments thousands of kilometres away.

Imagine arguing for three hours about the future of Iran when you cannot get your local government chairman to explain why the road outside your house looks like a crater.

And this applies to both sides. Whether you are waving an Iranian flag or defending Israel with religious enthusiasm, perhaps you should occasionally remember that you are Nigerian.

You can condemn injustice without becoming a foreign government's unpaid spokesperson. You can sympathise with Iranian civilians without worshipping their government. You can care about Israeli civilians without defending every decision made by Israel.

The problem begins when religion replaces independent thinking and disagreement becomes a religious offence.

So, if you are interested in acquiring Iranian or Israeli citizenship, the application process is apparently very simple.

Open a Facebook account, pick a side, and defend Iran or Israel like your life depends on it. Argue with strangers, insult anyone who disagrees with you, and make sure you never miss an opportunity to prove your loyalty.

You don't need a passport, an embassy appointment or proof of financial capacity. Just bring your data subscription and unlimited enthusiasm.

Never mind that you can barely afford three square meals, live in a country where insecurity and unemployment remain serious problems, and probably haven't seen your local representative since the last election.

Who knows? Perhaps after defending Iran or Israel for six months, someone will finally remember to send you your citizenship certificate.

Until then, please remember that you are still Nigerian. Unfortunately, Facebook arguments do not qualify as international relocation.
